Tier 1 Hyperscalers

The Big Three (+ Oracle) dominate total cloud spend but are not GPU specialists. Their GPU offerings are expensive on-demand, administratively heavy, and rarely the right fit for dedicated AI cluster deployments. They serve as the anchor tenants of neocloud providers, not their competitors.

Amazon Web Services (AWS)
Hyperscaler

The largest cloud provider globally. AWS's P4 and P5 instance families deliver NVIDIA A100 and H100 clusters for ML training and HPC workloads. After GCP's price move, AWS made a 44% H100 price cut in June 2025 — the most aggressive GPU discount in cloud history.

H100 On-Demand: ~$6.88/hr P5 instances (H100 8x) Global infra footprint
  • Buyer profile: Enterprise Fortune 500, existing AWS-native orgs
  • Pain points: High on-demand cost, complex reserved commitments, limited dedicated cluster options
  • vs. Nodestream: AWS can't offer dedicated bare-metal GPU clusters with financing
Microsoft Azure
Hyperscaler

Azure is the leading enterprise cloud and primary anchor tenant of CoreWeave and Nebius — paying $17–19B and signing similar arrangements with Crusoe. Azure's ND H100 v5 is the most expensive hyperscaler GPU offering at ~$12.29/hr on-demand. Azure's strategy is to lease neocloud capacity and resell it branded as Azure.

H100 On-Demand: ~$12.29/hr CoreWeave anchor tenant Nebius $17.4B contract
  • Buyer profile: Microsoft-ecosystem enterprises, OpenAI, regulated industries
  • Pain points: Highest list price in market; committed reservation complexity
  • vs. Nodestream: Azure has no owned GPU infra — it's repackaged neocloud capacity
Google Cloud Platform (GCP)
Hyperscaler

GCP is the most AI-native of the hyperscalers, with access to Google's own TPU architecture alongside NVIDIA H100/H200. GCP competes on price more aggressively than Azure. Google also operates the largest internal AI compute footprint on the planet, making them a competitor to neoclouds for frontier lab workloads.

H100 On-Demand: ~$11.06/hr TPU v5e / v5p available Preemptible: ~$2.10/hr
  • Buyer profile: AI research labs, Google Workspace enterprises, startup ecosystem
  • Pain points: TPU lock-in, complex pricing tiers, region availability gaps
  • vs. Nodestream: No dedicated cluster ownership; GPU-backed financing not available
Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI)
Hyperscaler

OCI has emerged as an aggressive GPU cloud player, signing a $300B+ arrangement with OpenAI and operating Crusoe's Stargate Abilene campus under a 15-year lease. OCI's BM.GPU.H100.8 bare-metal instances deliver dedicated H100 at aggressive pricing, and Oracle has made GPU infrastructure a central growth pillar into 2026.

OpenAI Stargate operator Crusoe Abilene lease partner Competitive bare-metal pricing
  • Buyer profile: Enterprises migrating from on-prem Oracle, AI labs via Stargate
  • Pain points: Smaller ecosystem than AWS/Azure; fewer regions
  • vs. Nodestream: OCI is a landlord, not an owner — compute capacity comes from Crusoe
Rep insight: When a prospect says "we use AWS/Azure" — ask them what they're paying per GPU-hour and whether they have a dedicated cluster or shared pool. Hyperscaler on-demand H100 runs $7–12/hr. Nodestream's dedicated reserved capacity at scale comes in significantly below that with ownership economics and financing options they can't access through any hyperscaler.
Tier 2 Neocloud Giants

Neocloud Giants are GPU-specialized cloud providers that have scaled to billion-dollar revenue. They sit between hyperscalers and mid-market players — offering dedicated GPU clusters, InfiniBand fabric, and managed AI infrastructure at meaningfully lower cost than hyperscalers. Their anchor customers are often the hyperscalers themselves.

CoreWeave
Neocloud Giant

The dominant neocloud. Originally a crypto mining operation that pivoted to GPU cloud in 2019. CoreWeave went public March 2025 (CRWV) and is the fastest cloud in history to $5B revenue. Their model: lease GPU clusters to hyperscalers and AI labs on multi-year contracts at premium pricing. Microsoft accounts for ~67% of 2025 revenue.

2025 Revenue: $5.1B 2026 Guide: $12–13B Backlog: $104B Capex 2026: $31–35B IPO Valuation: $23B (Mar 2025)
  • Customers: Microsoft, OpenAI, Meta, Anthropic — all anchor tenants
  • GPU fleet: NVIDIA H100, H200, GB200 clusters; InfiniBand fabric
  • Pricing: 10–15% premium over peers (SemiAnalysis) — buyers pay for reliability SLA
  • Risk: 67% Microsoft revenue concentration; $30B+ debt load
  • vs. Nodestream: CoreWeave is capacity-as-a-service; no owned land/power; no hardware financing
Nebius
Neocloud Giant

Amsterdam-headquartered neocloud (formerly Yandex Cloud International) operating GPU clusters in Europe and the US. Nebius has secured over $40B in contracted revenue from Microsoft ($17.4B) and Meta ($27B), making it one of the largest contracted neoclouds in the world despite being less recognized than CoreWeave.

Q2 2025 Revenue: $105M Q2 2025 Growth: +625% YoY Contracted: $40B+ ARR Target 2026: $7–9B
  • Key contracts: Microsoft $17.4–19.4B (Vineland NJ DC); Meta ~$27B
  • Rubin GPU deployment: 66,000 GPUs in Portugal (via Nscale partnership)
  • Financing: $775M senior secured debt raised July 2026
  • vs. Nodestream: European-heavy footprint; no OTC marketplace; no land/power ownership
Crusoe
Neocloud Giant

Founders Fund-backed energy-first AI infrastructure company. Crusoe's origin story is building data centers on stranded energy (flare gas) for cost-efficient GPU compute. They won the OpenAI Stargate Abilene campus — a $12B commitment to build and co-own the first major Stargate data center in Texas, leased to Oracle and used by OpenAI.

2024 Revenue: $276M 2025 Projected: ~$1B Valuation: $10B → $30B (2026) Stargate: $12B Abilene campus
  • Investors: NVIDIA, Fidelity, T. Rowe Price, Tiger Global, Salesforce Ventures, Founders Fund
  • Differentiation: Energy-first; stranded power assets; owns energy infrastructure
  • IPO path: Pre-IPO round at ~$30B valuation reported Bloomberg July 2026
  • vs. Nodestream: Crusoe is energy/infrastructure focused, not a sales-driven OTC/GPUaaS marketplace

Neocloud Giant

Over 10,000 customers including leading AI labs. Targeting IPO in H2 2026 following a pre-IPO raise at Mubadala Capital participation.

2025 Revenue: ~$1.2B Valuation: $5.9B Total Raised: $3.1B IPO Target: H2 2026

Nscale
Neocloud Giant

UK-based neocloud that reached a $14.6B valuation in March 2026 and is currently deploying 66,000 NVIDIA Rubin GPUs in Portugal for Microsoft. Nscale is one of the fastest-rising neoclouds in Europe, backed by significant contracted revenue from hyperscalers seeking European GPU capacity to meet data sovereignty requirements.

Valuation: $14.6B (Mar 2026) GPU deployment: 66K Rubin — Portugal Customer: Microsoft

Tier 3 Mid-Market Neoclouds

Mid-market neoclouds serve SMB AI teams, research groups, and cost-sensitive ML engineers. They compete primarily on price — offering H100 access at $1.50–$3/hr vs. hyperscaler list rates of $7–12/hr. Many operate marketplace models aggregating consumer and data center GPU supply. These are not your buyers — but your buyers may use these as a cost benchmark.

FluidStack
Mid-Market

UK-based GPU cloud with European and US footprints. FluidStack operates a hybrid model — 62% wholesale (dedicated clusters), 38% marketplace. Known for delivering 32–256 GPU clusters with non-blocking InfiniBand at competitive pricing and reasonable lead times. In late-stage fundraising at $18B valuation as of April 2026.

Funding: $450M equity (Jan 2026) Valuation talks: $18B H100: ~$1.90–2.50/hr
  • Sweet spot: 32–256 GPU cluster deployments, mid-scale training runs
  • Edge: InfiniBand fabric; EU and US presence; competitive pricing
  • vs. Nodestream: No hardware ownership; no OTC marketplace; no financing
RunPod
Mid-Market

Developer-first GPU cloud with per-second billing and serverless capabilities. RunPod operates two tiers: Secure Cloud (neocloud-grade data center GPUs) and Community Cloud (marketplace aggregating consumer GPUs). Supports AMD MI300X alongside NVIDIA. Popular with ML engineers for fast cold-starts and bursty inference workloads.

H100: ~$2.49/hr Per-second billing AMD MI300X + NVIDIA
  • Sweet spot: Research, ablations, bursty inference, small training runs
  • Limitation: Community Cloud GPUs vary in reliability; not enterprise-grade SLA
  • vs. Nodestream: No dedicated enterprise clusters; no financing; no large-scale reserved capacity
Vast.ai
Mid-Market

Pure marketplace model — the cheapest GPU hours on the market. Vast.ai surfaces consumer-grade and data center GPUs on a spot-like exchange, with H100 available from ~$1.74/hr. Buyers bid on available capacity, which creates price efficiency but zero reliability guarantees. Used heavily by cost-sensitive ML researchers and students.

H100 From: $1.74/hr Pure marketplace model Zero SLA on spot
  • Sweet spot: Cost-minimizing research; non-critical workloads; experimentation
  • Limitation: No enterprise SLA; mixed consumer/DC supply; not for production AI
  • vs. Nodestream: Completely different buyer — Vast.ai is not a competitor in enterprise deals
Hosted.ai
Mid-Market

Emerging GPU cloud provider targeting enterprise AI teams with dedicated NVIDIA cluster deployments. Hosted.ai focuses on managed GPU infrastructure with simplified onboarding, targeting teams that want the power of a dedicated neocloud cluster without the complexity of hyperscaler procurement. Growing presence in the US mid-market segment.

Focus: Dedicated clusters Segment: Enterprise mid-market GPU: NVIDIA H100 / H200
  • Sweet spot: Teams graduating from hyperscaler spot instances to dedicated capacity
  • vs. Nodestream: No hardware financing; no OTC; no land/power ownership model
HydraHost
Mid-Market

Specialist GPU hosting and HPC infrastructure provider serving enterprise and research markets. HydraHost delivers dedicated GPU server deployments and high-density HPC hosting with a focus on customer-controlled environments. Competes in the mid-market segment for buyers seeking more control than a public neocloud offers, with less complexity than self-built infrastructure.

Focus: Dedicated GPU hosting Model: Managed bare-metal Segment: Enterprise / Research
  • Sweet spot: Buyers needing dedicated, controlled GPU environments without building a DC
  • vs. Nodestream: No OTC marketplace; no GPU-backed financing; no land/power vertical integration
Tier 4 HPC Data Centers & Colo Operators

Traditional HPC facilities and colocation operators that have upgraded their infrastructure for GPU-dense AI workloads. These operators don't typically provide GPU compute as a service — they lease space, power, and cooling to neoclouds and enterprise buyers who bring their own hardware. AI-related colo leasing is up ~62% YoY as of 2026.

Equinix
HPC Colo

The world's largest data center operator with 260+ facilities across 33 countries. Equinix leases space and power to hyperscalers, neoclouds, and enterprises. Increasingly building AI-optimized facilities with liquid cooling and high-density power (50+ kW/rack). A key infrastructure layer underneath many neocloud deployments.

  • Space/power landlord to CoreWeave, hyperscalers, and enterprise AI teams
  • Now offering up to 100kW/rack liquid-cooled AI deployments
  • Not a GPU-as-a-Service provider — purely infrastructure and interconnect
Regional HPC Data Centers
HPC Colo

A broad category of Tier 2/3 data centers across North America that have repositioned for AI/HPC workloads. Average AI deployment rack density has reached ~50 kW in 2026 (vs. 10 kW for traditional IT). Providers like Rax Data, DataBank, QTS, and dozens of regional operators are competing for GPU cluster colocation contracts as neocloud expansion continues.

Avg rack density 2026: 50 kW AI colo growth: +62% YoY Liquid cooling: default for 50kW+
  • Buyers: neoclouds seeking regional power + space; enterprises with owned GPU fleets
  • Pain point: Power availability and energization timelines are the primary constraint
  • Nodestream connection: Nodestream's 23,000+ deeded acres with grid interconnects positions it above any colo operator
Key insight: The bottleneck for all Tier 4 operators in 2026 is the same as for neoclouds — energized megawatts. A data center without power is just a building. Nodestream's land and grid interconnect position is a fundamental differentiator over pure colo operators.
